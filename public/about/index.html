About us

We started with a map, not a plan.

Tumaini Community Trust was registered in 2025 by a group of teachers, a public health nurse and two agronomists who were tired of watching well-funded projects get built in the wrong place — and then abandoned. We are Kenyan-led, Kisumu-based, and we are launching our first full program year in 2026.

Our name means hope. It is not something we hand out; it is something a community builds when the water runs, the classroom has a roof, and the ledger adds up at the end of the month.

What we are here to do

Our mission

To help rural communities in western Kenya plan, build and maintain the services they rank as most urgent — and to keep the costs of that work visible to the people who fund it.

Our vision

A lake region where a child's chances do not depend on how far the nearest borehole is, and where local institutions — not visiting organisations — hold the knowledge.

Our values

  • Listen first. The community writes the brief.
  • Hire locally. Skills should stay in the ward.
  • Publish honestly. Failures included.
  • Do no harm. Safeguarding is not optional.

Method

Four rules we do not bend

These rules cost us speed. They are also the reason three community groups asked to work with us again before we had finished our first project.

  1. 01

    The community sets the priority

    We bring a menu and a budget range, never a decision. If a village chooses latrines over a borehole, that is what gets built.

  2. 02

    Every asset has a named caretaker

    Pumps, latrines and solar kits are handed over to a trained committee with a written maintenance plan and a spare-parts contact before we leave the site.

  3. 03

    We report on money, not feelings

    Quarterly updates publish line items: what was quoted, what was paid, who was contracted, and what is still outstanding.

  4. 04

    Children are never a photo opportunity

    Our child safeguarding and consent policy governs every image, interview and school visit on this site. Guardians sign; children can withdraw at any time.

Where we work

Three counties, seventeen communities

We deliberately stay small enough to visit every site by road in a single day.

Kisumu County

Accountability

Who answers for the money

Tumaini is governed by a five-member board of trustees that meets quarterly and keeps minutes. Our first audited accounts will be published in June 2026 — a year after registration, as the law requires. Until then, our interim financial statements are available on request.

Safeguarding and complaints: safeguarding@tumainitrust.org

Board
Five trustees: a retired head teacher, a paediatric nurse, an accountant, a water engineer and a farmer from Migori.
Audit
Annual external audit, plus a monthly bank reconciliation reviewed by two trustees who are not signatories.
Procurement
Three written quotes for any purchase above KES 50,000. Contractor names are published with each completed site.
Safeguarding
Every staff member and volunteer is vetted and signs our child protection policy before any community visit.
