Programs

Four programs, sized to what a community asked for.

Nothing on this page is a template. Each program below started as a request recorded in a village meeting, and each one carries a costed budget, a named caretaker and a reporting commitment.

Program 01

Classrooms & Scholarships

A child who drops out in Standard 7 rarely comes back. We work on the three things that push pupils out of school here: unsafe classrooms, unpaid levies and the long walk for water that lands on girls.

  • Classroom blocks repaired or rebuilt to county safety standards, with local masons on the payroll.
  • Bursaries paid directly to the school, never in cash, covering levies, uniforms and exam fees.
  • A "return to school" fund for girls who left during pregnancy or a family crisis, negotiated with the head teacher.
  • School sanitation blocks built alongside the classroom work — a toilet block is a retention intervention.

Program 02

Water, Sanitation & Hygiene

A borehole is not a project; it is a twenty-year commitment to a pump. We only drill where a water committee has already been formed, trained and equipped with a spare-parts contact.

  • Hydrogeological surveys before drilling, and we publish the siting report for every site.
  • Protected springs where the geology allows — cheaper, and easier for a community to maintain.
  • Two trained caretakers per water point, with a toolkit and a small maintenance fund held by the committee.
  • Hygiene clubs in every partner school, because hand-washing is what turns clean water into fewer clinic visits.

Program 03

Women's Livelihoods

Nine savings groups, roughly 220 women, meet every month with a shared ledger and a cash box. Our job is not to hand out money — it is to make sure the arithmetic is honest and the market is reachable.

  • Bookkeeping and mobile-money training for every group treasurer, delivered in Dholuo and Kiswahili.
  • Seed capital matched shilling-for-shilling against group savings — never a standalone grant.
  • Agronomy training with a rain gauge and a compost protocol that farmers can repeat without buying inputs.
  • Market days in Migori, with transport costs pooled so a single trader is not carrying the risk alone.

Program 04

Community Health Outreach

We do not run clinics. We help county health teams reach villages that a monthly outreach calendar has never quite managed to include — with transport, cold-chain boxes and community mobilisers.

  • Immunisation and growth-monitoring days run jointly with county community health promoters.
  • Antenatal referral support: transport vouchers for mothers who would otherwise miss a fourth visit.
  • Nutrition counselling tied to the harvest calendar, not a generic handout schedule.
  • Water quality testing after every new water point, so the health program and the WASH program share one dataset.

Measurement

How we know whether it worked

We collect three kinds of data, and we publish all three — including when the numbers disappoint.

Function, not construction

A pump that works in month twelve counts. We re-test every water point at six and twelve months and publish the result with a photo.

Attendance and retention

For schools we track enrolment, attendance and how many bursary pupils are still in class at the end of the year — not how many were enrolled in January.

Household income, self-reported

Savings group members report monthly earnings twice a year. We publish the median and the range, not just the best-performing group.
